The learner

Jordan Rivera · Class of 2027

About this record

This Transdisciplinary Learning Record shows how Jordan is growing across Franklin's nine transdisciplinary skills — the habits of mind we build in every subject, not just one.

Everything here comes from real coursework. There is no separate test for these skills. We draw on the assessments Jordan has already completed across their classes — each teacher's own rubric evaluation and written feedback — and gather the work that shows a skill in action.

  • No grades or averages. Proficiency appears in each teacher's own words ("Excelling," "Proficient," and so on), tied to a specific piece of work.
  • The featured work is real — each card below is an actual assignment.
  • Coverage is uneven on purpose. Some skills appear in more classes than others; a thin area shows where there's room to grow, or where we're still building ways to capture that skill.
  • Updated three times a year — a living picture of growth, separate from the official transcript.

Where this record is still thin

An honest record shows its own gaps. For this learner, two skills are under-evidenced — and that tells teachers exactly where to look next, rather than papering over it with a score.

  • Collaboration — this learner's collaborative projects (the Iliad Build, the Rube Goldberg machine) exist, but none were rubric-scored for collaboration itself. The teamwork happened; the record just can't see it yet.
  • Futures Thinking — visible only indirectly, through "transfer thinking" work that applies ideas to new contexts. No assessment named it directly.

About this prototype

This record was assembled automatically from work the student had already done — existing Canvas rubric evaluations and teacher comments across nine courses. No new grading happened, and no scores or averages were computed. That is the whole idea: making transdisciplinary growth visible should not become a second job for teachers.

Honest about coverage
